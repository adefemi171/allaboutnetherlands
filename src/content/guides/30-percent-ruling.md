This guide is for employees who were recruited from abroad to work in the Netherlands. The 30% ruling lets your employer pay part of your salary tax-free. The tax office (*Belastingdienst*) now calls it the expat scheme (*expatregeling*), but most people still say 30% ruling.

The rules have changed several times and change again in 2027. Every figure below says which year it is for.

## What it is

Living abroad costs extra. With the ruling, your employer may pay you up to 30% of your salary, including the allowance itself, as a tax-free allowance for those costs. You do not have to prove the costs.

- Your employer is not obliged to pay it, and may pay less than 30%. Agree it in writing before you start.
- Instead of the fixed percentage, your employer can reimburse your actual extra costs. The employer makes that choice once a year.
- There is a cap. In 2026 the tax-free allowance is at most €78,600 a year, which is reached at a salary of €262,000. If you work only part of the year, the cap is lower in proportion.
- School fees for an international school can be reimbursed tax-free on top.
- The option to be taxed partly as a non-resident no longer exists from the 2025 tax return. People who used the ruling before 2024 keep it up to the 2026 return.

## Who qualifies

You need to meet all of these:

1. **You are an employee.** Self-employed people do not qualify.
2. **You were recruited from abroad.** In the 24 months before your first working day, you lived more than 150 km from the Dutch border for more than 16 months. This rules out Belgium, Luxembourg and parts of Germany, France and the United Kingdom.
3. **You have specific expertise.** This is measured by your salary. In 2026 your taxable salary, not counting the tax-free allowance, must be more than €48,013 a year. If you are under 30 and have a Dutch master's degree or an equal foreign one, it must be more than €36,497 in 2026.
4. **You have a decision (*beschikking*) from the Belastingdienst.** Without it, your employer cannot apply the ruling.

The amounts go up every year. In 2025 the main requirement was €46,660.

Scientific researchers at designated institutions and doctors training as specialists have no salary requirement.

Keep an eye on your salary. If it falls below the requirement in any year, you lose the ruling from 1 January of that year and cannot get it back. The lower amount applies until the end of the month in which you turn 30.

## How and when to apply

1. Agree with your employer that the ruling applies. Put it in your contract or in an addendum.
2. Fill in the [application form](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontenten/themaoverstijgend/applications_and_forms/application-income-tax-and-national-insurance-30-facility) together. You both sign it. The form is called "Application Income tax and national insurance contributions Expat Scheme (30% facility)", with the year in front. In Dutch it is *Verzoek loonheffingen expatregeling (30%-regeling)*. It is a PDF. You cannot file it online. Print it, sign it with a pen and send it by post with the documents it asks for. The address is on the form. The Belastingdienst does not accept digitally signed forms.
3. Send it within 4 months of your first working day. The ruling then applies from your first day.
4. Wait for the decision. The Belastingdienst aims to answer within 8 weeks.

If you apply later than 4 months, the ruling only starts on the first day of the month after you applied. Until that date your employer may only reimburse your actual extra costs tax-free. The months you missed are also taken off your 5 years.

The Belastingdienst gives this example: you start on 1 February 2026 and apply on 15 June 2026. The ruling then starts on 1 July 2026.

## How long it lasts

The ruling lasts up to 5 years. Earlier periods in which you lived or worked in the Netherlands are deducted. Some periods do not count:

- periods that ended more than 25 years ago
- work in the Netherlands of no more than 20 days a year
- stays of no more than 6 weeks a year for holidays or family visits, plus one stay of up to 3 months in a row

The ruling ends at the end of the pay period after your last working day.

## What changes in 2027

From 1 January 2027 the maximum goes from 30% to 27%, and the salary requirement goes up for newer arrivals. What applies to you depends on when you first used the ruling.

| You first used the ruling | 2025 and 2026 | From 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In 2023 or earlier | 30% | 30%, current salary requirement |
| In 2024 | 30% | 27%, current salary requirement |
| In 2025 or later | 30% | 27%, higher salary requirement |

You are in the 2024 row if your employer applied the ruling for you no later than the last pay period of 2024. You then keep the current salary requirement for as long as your ruling runs.

The 27% is in the law. The text of the Wage Tax Act (*Wet op de loonbelasting 1964*) that applies from 1 January 2027 says 27% where it now says 30%.

**The higher salary requirement.** It was set at €50,436 at 2024 prices, or €38,338 if you are under 30 with a master's degree. The current requirement was €46,107 and €35,048 in 2024. All amounts are adjusted for inflation each year. In October 2026 the Belastingdienst gave €51,899 and €39,450 for the higher requirement from 1 January 2027, and said these will be adjusted once more. So the final 2027 amounts will be higher. They were not yet published on 15 November 2026. Check the [Belastingdienst page](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/en/individuals/content/coming-to-work-in-the-netherlands-30-percent-facility) for them.

**The cap.** The cap is tied to the maximum pay for top officials. That maximum goes from €262,000 in 2026 to €273,000 in 2027. From 2027 the law caps the allowance at 27% of it, which comes to €73,710 a year. The Belastingdienst had not yet published the 2027 cap on 15 November 2026.

## Changing jobs

You can take the ruling with you, for the time that is left.

- **Same group of companies.** Your decision stays valid.
- **A company takeover.** The ruling moves to the new employer by itself.
- **A new employer.** You must start the new job within 3 months of leaving the old one. You and the new employer apply again, and you must again meet the salary requirement. Apply within 4 months of starting, so the ruling runs from your first day there.

If the gap between jobs is longer than 3 months, the ruling ends.
