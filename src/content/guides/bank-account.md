This guide is for anyone who has just arrived and needs a Dutch bank account. It explains what banks ask for, whether you need a BSN first, and how people pay here day to day. It also explains what to do if no bank accepts you.

## Why you need one

Your employer pays your salary into a bank account. Rent, energy and your [health insurance](/guides/health-insurance/) are usually paid by transfer or direct debit. Most payments in shops are by card, and Dutch web shops expect you to pay through your bank.

## Get your BSN first

If you pay tax in the Netherlands, you need a citizen service number (BSN) to open a bank account. Banks must report account data to the tax office (*Belastingdienst*), and they use your BSN for that.

So the usual order is: [register with your municipality and get your BSN](/guides/register-and-get-a-bsn/), then open the account.

Some banks let you open an account before you have a BSN. You then have to give the number later, within a fixed time:

- ABN AMRO: you can open an account without a BSN. If the bank does not have your BSN within 90 days, it blocks the account. After 120 days without a BSN, it ends the account.
- bunq: you can start without a BSN and must give it within 90 days.

Other banks have their own rules. Ask the bank before you apply.

## What banks ask for

Banks set their own rules, so the list differs a little. Expect to need:

- a valid passport or national identity card. ABN AMRO, for example, also accepts a Dutch residence permit as ID
- your BSN
- your address in the Netherlands. Most banks want a Dutch address. Some online banks accept a foreign address for a short time

If your passport is from outside the EU or EEA, a bank may also ask for proof that you may stay in the Netherlands. ABN AMRO, for example, asks for proof of residence in the Netherlands first. Without it you can open the account, but you cannot use it yet.

Banks are required by law to check who you are. They may also ask where your money comes from, or ask for more documents. This is normal and is part of the rules against money laundering.

Many banks let you apply in their app, with a check of your ID on your phone. Others ask you to come to a branch. When you compare banks, look at the monthly fee, whether the app and customer service are in English, and whether you can deposit cash.

## How you pay day to day

### In shops

You pay with a debit card (*pinpas* or *betaalpas*). The money leaves your account straight away. You hold the card against the terminal, and for amounts up to €50 you usually do not need your PIN. After several payments without PIN that add up to €100, the terminal asks for your PIN again. Abroad, other limits can apply. You can also add the card to your phone or watch and pay with that.

Cash is used less and less. In 2025, 83% of payments at the checkout were by card and 17% were in cash.

Dutch debit cards are changing:

| | Old cards | New cards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logo | Maestro or V PAY | Debit Mastercard or Visa Debit |
| Card number | No | Yes, 16 digits plus a security code |
| Paying online with the card | No | Yes |
| Outside Europe | Limited | Accepted in more places |

Banks are replacing old cards step by step, and many old cards are still in use. If you get one, your bank will send the new type by itself later. All Dutch payment terminals accept the new cards. Credit cards are not accepted everywhere, so do not rely on one.

### Online

Dutch web shops use iDEAL. You choose your bank, approve the payment in your banking app, and the money is sent at once. It comes free with every Dutch current account.

iDEAL is being replaced by Wero, a European payment system. This is where things stand now:

- Since January 2026, the logo shows both names: iDEAL | Wero.
- Since 26 October 2026, customers of the Dutch banks that take part can pay with Wero in web shops. These include ABN AMRO, ASN Bank, bunq, ING, Knab, Rabobank, Revolut and Triodos Bank. Web shops now move over step by step.
- By the end of 2027, every web shop should have moved. After that, the iDEAL name disappears step by step.

You do not have to do anything. You keep paying through your own bank, in the same way. You do not need to sign up for a new service. Wero and your bank never ask for personal or bank details outside your bank's own app or website.

### Paying each other

To split a bill, people send a payment request (*betaalverzoek*). You make one in your banking app and send it as a link or QR code in a message. The other person pays through their own bank, also if it is a different bank. Before you pay a request, check that it really comes from the person you know.

## If a bank refuses you

Banks may choose their customers. If one says no, try another bank first.

If that does not work, you can apply for a basic payment account (*basisbetaalrekening*). Anyone who lives legally in the EU and cannot get a normal account has a right to one. It is a simple account with a debit card, transfers and direct debits. It usually has no overdraft and no credit card. Betaalvereniging Nederland has an online tool that shows where to apply.

The bank must open the account within 10 working days after it has received your complete application. It may refuse, for example if:

- you already have a Dutch payment account, or have applied for one elsewhere
- you cannot show a real reason to have an account here, such as living, working or studying in the Netherlands

If you disagree, complain to the bank first. After that you can go to Kifid, the financial complaints institute.

## Is your money safe?

Money in a current or savings account is protected up to €100,000 per person per bank. Some banks that operate here fall under the guarantee scheme of another EU country. That scheme protects the same amount. Your bank can tell you which scheme applies.
