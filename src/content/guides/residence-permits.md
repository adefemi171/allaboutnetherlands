This guide is for anyone who wants to live in the Netherlands for more than 90 days. It explains who needs a residence permit, how the application works and which routes exist. For the full conditions, follow the route to its page on the immigration service (IND) website.

## Who needs a residence permit

**EU, EEA and Swiss citizens** do not need one. Your passport or ID card is proof that you may live and work here. You do not register with the IND, only with the municipality if you stay more than 4 months. See [Register and get a BSN](/guides/register-and-get-a-bsn/).

**Everyone else** needs a residence permit to stay longer than 90 days. The IND (*Immigratie- en Naturalisatiedienst*) decides on every application.

## The MVV and the combined procedure

Most people also need an entry visa called the MVV (*machtiging tot voorlopig verblijf*). It is a sticker in your passport. You apply for the MVV and the residence permit in one go. This is the entry and residence procedure (*toegang en verblijf*, TEV).

You do not need an MVV if you are a national of Australia, Canada, Japan, Monaco, New Zealand, South Korea, the United Kingdom, the United States or Vatican City. You still need the residence permit itself.

The procedure with an MVV goes like this:

1. Your sponsor in the Netherlands (employer, school or partner) applies to the IND. If no sponsor applies for you, you apply at the Dutch embassy or consulate in your country of origin or the country where you live.
2. The IND decides and sends a letter.
3. After a positive decision, you make an appointment at the embassy within 3 months. Your passport must be valid for at least 6 more months. The embassy places the MVV sticker in your passport. It can take up to 10 working days before the sticker is ready.
4. The MVV is valid for 90 days. The 90 days start on the issue date printed on the sticker. Travel within that time.
5. You collect your residence permit card at an IND desk. The MVV must still be valid on the day you collect it.

The IND advises you not to stay in the Netherlands while the MVV application is running.

### Applying when you are already in the Netherlands

The IND route pages say the following. They differ per route.

- **Highly skilled migrant.** If you are already in the Netherlands, your employer must apply within 3 months after you travelled here. You may start working once your employer has a positive decision that says so. You do not have to wait for the card.
- **EU Blue Card.** Your employer must apply within 3 months after you travel to the Netherlands.
- **Self-employed.** You can apply by post within 3 months after you have travelled to the Netherlands.
- **Orientation year.** You can apply online if you live in the Netherlands, graduated or did research here, hold a valid permit for that, are registered at the municipality and have a BSN. You may start working during the application in some cases, for example if you sent a copy of your Dutch diploma. You first need a residence endorsement sticker in your passport.
- **Partner.** There is a procedure without MVV. You apply online or by post.
- **Study.** The IND page only describes the procedure with an MVV.

A residence endorsement sticker (*verblijfsaantekening*) is proof that you may wait for the decision in the Netherlands. You can ask the IND for one after you applied for a first permit without an MVV.

## The main routes

Fees change every 1 January. The 2027 fees were published in the Government Gazette (*Staatscourant*) and apply from 1 January 2027. The salary and income amounts below are for 2026. The IND had not published the 2027 amounts on 15 November 2026.

| Route | Who applies | IND fee in 2026 | IND fee from 1 January 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Highly skilled migrant | Your employer (recognised sponsor only) | €423 | €438 |
| EU Blue Card | Your employer, or you | €423 | €438 |
| Orientation year | You | €254 | €254 |
| Study | Your school or university only | €254 | €263 |
| Partner | Your partner in the Netherlands, or you | €254 | €263 |
| Self-employed | You | €423 | €438 |

- **Highly skilled migrant (*kennismigrant*).** Only an employer that is a recognised sponsor can apply. You cannot apply yourself. In 2026 your gross monthly salary, not counting holiday allowance, must be at least €5,942 if you are 30 or older and €4,357 if you are under 30. A lower amount of €3,122 applies after an orientation year or within 3 years of graduating. The permit lasts as long as your contract, up to 5 years.
- **EU Blue Card.** Your employer does not have to be a recognised sponsor. You need a higher-education diploma from a course of at least 3 years, or 5 years of relevant work experience, and a contract of at least 6 months. The IND recommends that your employer applies in the Netherlands. You may also apply to the IND yourself. The 2026 salary requirement is €5,942 gross per month. A reduced amount of €4,754 applies to graduates of a higher education programme who apply within 3 years after graduating or getting their doctorate.
- **Orientation year (*zoekjaar*).** You apply yourself. From abroad you apply at the Dutch embassy or consulate. For people who graduated in the Netherlands, or from a top-ranked foreign university, in the last 3 years. You get 1 year to look for work and may take any job. It cannot be extended.
- **Study.** Only your school or university can apply for you. You must show you have enough money to live on (€1,130.77 per month in 2026) and earn at least 50% of your credits each year. You may work 16 hours a week, or full-time in June, July and August. Your employer needs a work permit for you.
- **Partner.** Your partner in the Netherlands usually applies, online or by post. You can also apply yourself, for example at the Dutch embassy or consulate abroad. You must both be 21 or older and live together. Your partner must earn at least €2,337.00 gross per month, not counting holiday allowance (amount for 1 July to 31 December 2026). Most people who need an MVV must first pass the civic integration exam abroad.
- **Self-employed.** You apply yourself. With an MVV you apply at the Dutch embassy or consulate and then send the application to the IND within 3 weeks. Your business must serve an essential interest of the Dutch economy, which is assessed with a points system. The permit is valid for up to 2 years.

### Planned stricter rules for highly skilled migrants

In 2025 the government announced stricter rules for highly skilled migrants. It wants to raise the salary requirement for people under 30 to 1.1 times the average gross annual salary. It also wants tougher rules for recognised sponsors. A sponsor would lose its recognition after 3 or more fines in 4 years, or if it has employed no highly skilled migrant for 2 years.

These plans are not in force. Business.gov.nl says the start date is not yet known and that the proposals have not passed parliament. It expects the salary thresholds to rise by more than the usual yearly indexation in 2027. The IND had not published 2027 amounts on 15 November 2026.

## Recognised sponsors

A recognised sponsor (*erkend referent*) is an employer or school that the IND has approved. The highly skilled migrant and study routes require one. It is also faster. The IND aims to decide on a complete application within 2 weeks, against a legal limit of 90 days for highly skilled migrants and 60 days for students. Before you sign a contract, look up the employer in the [public register of recognised sponsors](https://ind.nl/en/public-register-recognised-sponsors).

## After the decision

1. **Give your biometrics.** These are your fingerprints, photo and signature. The IND letter tells you whether you still need to give them. If so, book a free appointment at an IND desk and bring your passport and the appointment code. Highly skilled migrants should do this within 2 weeks of the decision.
2. **Collect your permit.** The IND tries to have the card ready within 2 weeks after your biometrics. The letter says where to collect it.
3. **Take a tuberculosis test if the IND asks for one.** You do it at the municipal health service (GGD) within 3 months of receiving your permit.
4. **Register with the municipality.** See [Register and get a BSN](/guides/register-and-get-a-bsn/).
