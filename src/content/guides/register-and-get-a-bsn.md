This guide explains how to register your address with the municipality (*gemeente*) and get a citizen service number (*burgerservicenummer*, BSN). You need to do this if you come to live in the Netherlands for more than 4 months. Your employer, your [bank](/guides/bank-account/), your [health insurer](/guides/health-insurance/) and [DigiD](/guides/digid/) all ask for the BSN, so do this first.

## Who must register, and by when

If you will stay in the Netherlands for more than 4 months, you must register in the Personal Records Database (*Basisregistratie Personen*, BRP). You do this at the municipality where you live, within 5 days of arriving. You get your BSN when you register for the first time.

The BRP is the government's record of who lives where. Only organisations with a public task, such as the tax office, can use your data.

Two conditions apply:

- **You must be in the Netherlands legally.** If you need a residence permit, you may have to arrange that first. See [Residence permits](/guides/residence-permits/).
- **You need an address.** If you do not have one yet, see [Find a home](/guides/find-a-home/). If you have no home address at all, see [If you have no home address](#if-you-have-no-home-address) below.

Check your municipality's website as soon as you arrive. The Hague, Rotterdam, Utrecht, Eindhoven and Groningen all ask you to make an appointment. Amsterdam lets you walk in.

Registration is free. The national identity data agency (RvIG) says that registering in the BRP never costs money. The Hague, Rotterdam and Groningen say the same on their own pages.

## What to bring

Everyone who registers must come in person. That includes your partner and your children, also babies.

Amsterdam, The Hague, Utrecht, Eindhoven and Groningen ask for the same basic documents:

- A valid passport or national identity card. A driving licence is not accepted.
- If you are not an EU, EEA or Swiss citizen: your residence permit or other proof from the IND that you may stay, such as the sticker in your passport or the IND letter.
- Proof of your address: your rental contract or purchase contract. If you live in someone else's home, bring a signed consent form from the owner or main resident and a copy of their ID.
- Your original birth certificate.
- If they apply to you: your marriage or divorce certificates, and your children's birth certificates.

The details differ per municipality:

| Municipality | What its page says |
| --- | --- |
| The Hague | Certificates are not required to register. Bring them if you were born, married or divorced abroad, and the municipality will record them. |
| Utrecht | Bring original documents from abroad for yourself and your children. You are not registered before the day your rental or purchase contract starts. |
| Eindhoven | Bring originals, not copies. If you come from Belgium, also bring proof that you deregistered there. |
| Groningen | Bring your birth certificate if it was not registered before, and your marriage or divorce certificate if you are married or divorced. |
| Rotterdam | You see the list of documents when you start the online registration form. |

Utrecht, Eindhoven and Groningen also ask for proof of deregistration if you come from Aruba, Curaçao, Sint Maarten, Bonaire, Sint Eustatius or Saba.

Eindhoven says it can ask for extra information and proof in some situations. Always read your own municipality's list before you go.

## Certificates: legalisation and translation

Municipalities want original certificates, not copies. Two extra rules can apply.

**Language.** Certificates must be in Dutch, English, French or German. If yours are in another language, you need a translation by a sworn translator. A translator in the Netherlands must be sworn in by a Dutch court.

**Legalisation.** This is proof that a foreign document is genuine. What you need depends on the country that issued it:

| Where the document is from | What you usually need |
| --- | --- |
| An EU country | No legalisation. Ask the issuing office for a multilingual standard form, so you do not need a translation. |
| A country that uses the apostille | One stamp (the apostille) from the authorities in that country. |
| Any other country | Two steps: first the authorities in that country, then the Dutch embassy there. |

Look up your country on Netherlands Worldwide to see the exact rule. Legalisation is almost always done in the country that issued the document, and it can take a long time. Start before you move, as described in [Move to the Netherlands](/guides/move-to-the-netherlands/).

Do not delay your registration because a certificate is missing. In The Hague, for example, certificates are not required to register, and you can hand them in later.

## What happens at the appointment

1. An official checks your identity and your documents.
2. Your personal details and your address go into the BRP.
3. You get your BSN. How fast depends on the municipality.

| Municipality | When you get the BSN |
| --- | --- |
| Groningen | At the appointment, if this is the first time you live in the Netherlands. |
| The Hague | By post. The registration is usually processed within 4 weeks. Then you get a confirmation letter with your BSN. |
| Eindhoven | By post. You get a confirmation letter with your BSN within 4 weeks. |
| Rotterdam | The registration is usually processed within 4 weeks after you have handed in all documents. You are told in writing. |
| Utrecht | It may take 4 weeks before your registration is in the BRP. |

If you need the BSN quickly for your employer or your bank, ask at the appointment when you will get it.

If the municipality cannot confirm your identity or your right to stay, it may investigate first.

## If you have no home address

If you have no home address, you can register at a correspondence address (*briefadres*). This is an address where your official post arrives. It is not where you live. These are the national rules:

- The address must be in the Netherlands. It is the address of a family member or someone you know, or of an organisation that the municipality has appointed. A P.O. box is not allowed.
- You register at the municipality where the address is.
- Bring your own ID, a written statement of why you need a correspondence address, signed written consent from someone who lives at the address, and their ID or a copy of it.
- The municipality checks whether you really have no home address.
- A correspondence address is temporary. The municipality often agrees with you how long you can use it.

A municipality may not add its own conditions on top of the ones in the law. Registering at a correspondence address is free.

## If you stay less than 4 months

You do not register as a resident. You can register as a non-resident in the Non-residents Records Database (*Registratie Niet-ingezetenen*, RNI), with your address abroad. You also get a BSN this way.

- There are 19 municipalities with an RNI desk. Dutch, EU, EEA and Swiss citizens can use any of them.
- All other nationalities can only register at the desks in Breda and Venlo. This rule started in January 2026.
- Make an appointment and come in person with a valid passport or identity card.
- Children aged 16 or older register themselves. For younger children, a parent brings the child's passport or ID and birth certificate.
- You leave with a letter that states your BSN.

## When you move or leave

**Moving within the Netherlands.** Report your new address to your new municipality. You can do this from 4 weeks before the move until 5 days after it. If you are late, the municipality can fine you up to €325.

**Leaving the Netherlands.** You must deregister if you will be abroad for more than 8 months within a 12-month period. The months do not have to be in a row, and it does not matter if you keep your home here. Deregister in the 5 days before you leave.

How you deregister depends on your municipality. Government.nl says you do it in person, but several large municipalities also offer other ways:

- Eindhoven, Utrecht and Rotterdam let you deregister online with DigiD.
- Utrecht and Eindhoven also accept a printed form by post, or an appointment at the counter.
- The Hague lets you deregister online, by post or at the counter.

If some family members stay behind at the address, you must always deregister in person. Deregistering is free in The Hague and Eindhoven.

Ask for proof of deregistration. Your BSN stays valid, and your data moves to the RNI.
