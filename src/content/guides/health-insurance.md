This guide explains Dutch basic health insurance (*basisverzekering*): who must have it, by when, what it covers and what it costs. It is for anyone who comes to live or work in the Netherlands, including students, people who do not work, and people who are still insured in another country.

## Who must have it, and by when

Everyone who lives or works in the Netherlands must have Dutch basic health insurance. A foreign, travel or international "expat" policy does not count.

Take out the insurance no later than 4 months after you arrive. If you do, your cover starts on the day you arrived. If you are later, your cover does not go back. You pay premium only from the day you take out the insurance, and you pay for any care before that day yourself.

You choose the insurer. Every insurer must accept you for the basic insurance.

To take out the insurance you normally need your [BSN](/guides/register-and-get-a-bsn/), a bank account number and a home address. Insurers must record your BSN.

## Check your situation

| Your situation | What applies |
| --- | --- |
| You work for a Dutch employer | You must take out Dutch insurance. |
| You only study here | Usually you do not take Dutch basic insurance. Make sure you have other cover, for example from your home country. |
| You study and have a paid job | You must take out Dutch insurance, also with a few hours or a zero-hours contract. For internships, see below. |
| You live here and do not work | [Register with your municipality](/guides/register-and-get-a-bsn/). The Social Insurance Bank (SVB) decides if you count as a resident. If you do, you must insure. |
| Your employer is outside the Netherlands | You may be allowed to stay insured in your home country. Ask the SVB. |
| You are insured in another EU or EEA country or Switzerland, and do not work here | You can ask the SVB for a certificate that you are not insured here. See "Insured in another EU country" below. |
| You are waiting for your residence permit | Insurers often cannot accept you yet. Ask the SVB for an assessment. |
| Children under 18 | No premium, but you must register them with an insurer. |

If you are not sure, ask the SVB for an assessment of your position under the Long-term Care Act (*Wet langdurige zorg*, Wlz). It can take up to 8 weeks. The result tells you whether you must have Dutch insurance.

**Internships.** The SVB changed the rules for paid internships on 1 September 2025:

- You live in the EU, the EEA, Switzerland or a treaty country: you must insure if you fall under the Dutch employee insurance schemes during the internship. Ask your internship company or the UWV.
- You live in another country: you must insure if your internship allowance is at least the Dutch minimum wage. An expense allowance counts as pay. Free board and lodging sometimes counts too.

The Dienst Toeslagen page for foreign students only gives the minimum wage rule. The SVB decides, so follow the SVB.

**Insured in another EU country.** If you live here on a pension or benefit from another EU or EEA country or Switzerland, you are normally not insured under the Wlz. The country that pays you can give you an S1 form. With it you can register for healthcare in the country where you live. The SVB says you may be able to take out a treaty policy (*Verdragspolis*). Your care in the Netherlands is then paid as if you were insured here. The SVB refers you to the insurer CZ for this.

A European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) from your home country only covers necessary care during a temporary stay. It does not replace Dutch insurance.

## What the basic package covers

The government decides what is in the basic package. It is the same at every insurer.

- Covered: the GP, hospital and specialist care, most medicines, mental health care, midwife and maternity care, ambulance, and dental care for children under 18.
- Not covered, or only a little: dental care for adults, most physiotherapy for adults, glasses and contact lenses.

Policies differ in price and in which hospitals and providers they pay in full. Check this before you choose.

## What it costs

**Premium.** Each insurer sets its own monthly premium. In 2026 the average was €157 a month. Premiums change every January.

For 2027 the government expected an average of €169 a month. Insurers had to publish their 2027 premiums by 12 November 2026. One example: the insurer DSW charges €173.25 a month in 2027.

**Deductible (*eigen risico*).** From age 18, you pay the first part of your care costs each year yourself.

| Year | Compulsory deductible |
| --- | --- |
| 2026 | €385 |
| 2027 | €400 |

A visit to the GP, midwife care, maternity care and district nursing do not count towards the deductible. Medicines, blood tests and hospital care do. You can choose a voluntary extra deductible of €100, €200, €300, €400 or €500 a year in return for a lower premium. These amounts are fixed in the Health Insurance Act itself. They are not set again each year.

The €400 for 2027 was set by a regulation in the Staatscourant of 25 September 2026. Some Rijksoverheid pages still show €385 for 2027. The regulation is the law.

## Supplementary insurance

Supplementary insurance (*aanvullende verzekering*) is optional. It can pay for dental care, physiotherapy or glasses. An insurer may refuse you for it. You may buy it from a different insurer than your basic insurance.

## Healthcare allowance

If your income is low, the government pays part of your premium. This is the healthcare allowance (*zorgtoeslag*). The conditions for 2026:

- you are 18 or older and have Dutch health insurance
- your income is at most €40,857 a year if you are single, or €51,142 together with a partner
- your assets on 1 January 2026 were at most €146,011 if single, or €184,633 together

The allowance in 2026 is at most €129 a month if you are single, or €246 a month together with a partner. The higher your income, the less you get.

What is known for 2027:

- the asset limits go down: at most €119,122 on 1 January 2027 if single, or €158,748 together
- the government expects a maximum of €140 a month for a single person
- the income limits for 2027 are not published yet

Do you get the allowance now, and will your assets be above the new limit? Report this in Mijn toeslagen by 8 December 2026.

You apply at the Benefits office (*Dienst Toeslagen*) with your BSN and [DigiD](/guides/digid/). You can apply for 2026 until 31 December 2027. Report changes in your income.

## Register with a GP

The GP (*huisarts*) is your first contact for almost all care. You need a referral from the GP to see a specialist. Register with a practice near your home soon after you arrive. Do not wait until you are ill.

You may choose your own GP. A practice can refuse you if it is full or if you live too far away. If you cannot find one, ask your insurer for help. For urgent problems at night and at weekends, call the out-of-hours GP service (*huisartsenpost*).

## If you stay uninsured

The CAK checks who is registered at a Dutch address without insurance. If that is you, you get a letter. You then have 3 months to do one of two things:

1. Take out Dutch basic insurance.
2. Ask the SVB for a Wlz assessment, if you think you do not need Dutch insurance.

If you ask for a Wlz assessment, the SVB answers within 8 weeks. If the answer is that you must insure, you get 6 more weeks to do it.

If you do nothing, you get a fine. In 2026 the fine is €529.74. You must still take out insurance. If you do not do so within 3 months after the first fine, you get a second fine of the same amount.

If you are still uninsured after the second fine, the CAK takes out insurance for you:

- You pay the premium to the CAK for 12 months. It is €172.70 a month.
- If you have income in the Netherlands, the CAK takes the premium from your salary. If not, the CJIB sends you a bill every month.
- You cannot change insurer during these 12 months.
