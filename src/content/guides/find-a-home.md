This guide is for anyone who needs to rent a home in the Netherlands. It covers how the market is split, where to search, what landlords ask, deposits and contracts, scams and rent disputes.

Start early. Housing is short, and you need an address before you can register and get a BSN.

## How the rental market is split

There are three segments. Every home gets points for its quality under the points system (*woningwaarderingsstelsel*), and in two of the three segments the points set the maximum rent.

| Segment | Basic rent, contracts starting in 2026 | Contracts starting in 2027 | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Social rent (*sociale huur*) | Up to €932.93 a month | Up to €959.71 a month | Regulated. Mostly let by housing associations to people under an income limit |
| Mid-rent (*middenhuur*) | €932.93 to €1,228.07 a month | €959.71 to €1,263.32 a month | Regulated. The points set the maximum rent |
| Free sector (*vrije sector*) | Above €1,228.07 a month | Above €1,263.32 a month | No maximum rent. Only the yearly increase is capped |

The limit that counts is the one for the year your contract starts. The 2027 limits are the 2026 limits raised by 2.87% inflation.

Regulated rent means the landlord cannot ask more than the points allow. Rooms are always regulated. Since 1 January 2025 the landlord must give you the points count with the contract.

Social housing goes through a housing association (*woningcorporatie*). In 2026 the income limit is €51,537 for one person and €56,910 for a larger household. The 2027 income limits were not published on 15 November 2026.

By law a housing association must let at least 92.5% of the social homes that come free each year to households under the income limit. It may let up to 7.5% to households with a higher income. The association, the municipality and the tenants' organisation can agree locally to raise that free share to at most 15%. Then at least 85% still goes to households under the limit.

You sign up and respond to listings, and the home usually goes to whoever has been registered longest. The wait can be a few years, so expect to rent privately at first. If you plan to stay, sign up anyway. Your municipality can tell you where.

## Where people search

- **Listing websites.** Funda and Pararius are two well-known examples for private rentals.
- **Rental agents.** An agent who works for the landlord may not charge you a fee, whatever name they give it.
- **Housing association websites.** These are regional. WoningNet is one example.
- **Social media groups.** Be careful here. This is where fraudsters find it easiest to work.

## What landlords ask for

Expect to show:

- your passport or ID card
- proof of income, such as payslips or a bank statement that shows your salary
- a statement from your previous landlord (*verhuurdersverklaring*)

A rental agent may ask for income details once you are likely to sign the contract. A housing association may ask for them only when it really needs them, for example when you sign. An agent may not ask for your BSN, nationality or ethnicity. A landlord may not ask about your ethnic or cultural background, religion, political views, sexual orientation or health.

There is no official figure for the income a private landlord requires. The income limits for social housing apply only to housing associations, not to private landlords. A landlord may ask for your total net monthly income and proof of it. A landlord must publish how tenants are selected and use criteria that do not discriminate. If the home goes to someone else, the landlord must explain why. So ask for the income requirement before you view.

## Deposit and contract

- **Deposit (*waarborgsom* or *borg*).** The maximum is 2 months of basic rent (*kale huur*). Pay by bank transfer. You get it back within 14 days of the end of the contract. If the landlord deducts costs, they have 30 days and must list the costs in writing.
- **Contract.** It must be in writing. Since 1 July 2024 new tenants normally get a contract with no end date. A temporary contract of up to 2 years is only allowed in the situations listed below.
- **Extra costs.** Key money (*sleutelgeld*) is not allowed.
- **Rent increases.** The rent may go up once a year. The maximum in 2026 is 4.1% for social rent (from 1 July 2026), 6.1% for mid-rent and 4.4% in the free sector. The 2027 percentages were not published on 15 November 2026. They are set and announced each December.

A landlord may only offer you a temporary contract if you:

- are a student and rent for your studies in a different municipality from the one where you live
- live somewhere else for a while because your own home is being renovated or worked on
- come from a shelter (*maatschappelijke opvang*) or are in a social emergency
- get a second chance after an earlier contract was ended, for example because of nuisance
- lived with a tenant who has died and do not continue the tenancy as co-tenant (this does not apply to orphans aged 16 to 27 in a housing association home)
- are divorcing, no longer live with your partner and rent for a while to stay close to your children
- work for a while on one of the Wadden Islands but live in a municipality on the mainland
- are a permit holder (*statushouder*) who comes straight from a COA reception centre and is waiting for permanent housing

A temporary contract always has an end date and ends on that date. The landlord must tell you by letter 1 to 3 months before. You may leave earlier. If the contract is extended, it becomes a contract with no end date.

There is one more case. An owner who is away for a while, for example to work abroad, may let the home for an agreed period (*tussenhuur* or *diplomatenclausule*). You leave when the owner comes back, and you cannot end this contract early.

## Check that you can register at the address

You must register with the municipality at the address where you live. The municipality asks for your rental contract, or a signed consent form from the main occupant if you sublet or share.

Ask before you sign whether you can register, and get the answer in writing. By law you must register at the address where you actually live. The Council of State ruled in 2022 that this duty stays even if the landlord gives no permission, and that registration at a home address does not depend on that permission. Still, a landlord who forbids it is one to avoid. See [Register and get a BSN](/guides/register-and-get-a-bsn/).

## Common scams

Rental fraud is a growing problem. Watch for these signs:

- the rent is low for the place
- the landlord is abroad and cannot show the home
- you are asked to pay a deposit or first rent before you get a contract and keys
- the bank account is foreign or in someone else's name
- you are pressed to pay quickly

Search the address, the photos and the landlord's name online. Do not pay before you have seen the home and signed. A viewing alone is no guarantee.

## If you disagree about the rent

The rent tribunal (*Huurcommissie*) rules on rent levels, maintenance and service charges. It costs you €25, and you get that back if you win. It can only lower your rent if the points put your home in a regulated segment. For contracts signed on or after 1 July 2024 that means up to 186 points.

1. Estimate the points for your home with the Huurcommissie's [rent check](https://huurprijscheck.huurcommissie.nl/en/start/rent-check).
2. If the rent is above the maximum, ask the landlord in writing to lower it.
3. If they refuse, start a case in MijnHuurcommissie. You log in with [DigiD](/guides/digid/).

Do this within 6 months of the start of your contract. Then you can claim back what you overpaid. For contracts signed on or after 1 July 2024 you can also ask later, but the lower rent then only applies from that point on. Keep paying the agreed rent until there is a ruling.

For help, look for a local tenant support team (*huurteam*) or the Juridisch Loket. Every municipality also has a reporting point (*meldpunt*) for landlords who break the rules.
