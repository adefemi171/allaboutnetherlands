This checklist is for anyone moving to the Netherlands for more than 4 months, to work, study or join a partner. It puts the steps in the order that works, because several of them depend on the one before. Each step links to a guide with the details.

Coming from Nigeria or elsewhere in West Africa? The regional guide covers document legalisation, inviting family and the Naija @ Netherlands community directory. [Moving from Nigeria or West Africa](/guides/moving-from-nigeria-or-west-africa/)

## Before you leave

1. **Check whether you need a residence permit.** EU, EEA and Swiss citizens do not need one. Everyone else does, and for most people the application starts before they travel. See [Residence permits](/guides/residence-permits/).
2. **Start looking for a home.** You need an address where you can register, and finding one takes time. See [Find a home](/guides/find-a-home/).
3. **Gather your documents.** You will need a valid passport or ID card and your birth certificate. Bring your marriage or divorce certificate if you have one, and your children's birth certificates. The large municipalities ask for the same basic documents, but the details differ. The Hague, for example, does not require certificates to register, and Eindhoven wants originals, not copies. Read your own municipality's list, and see [Register and get a BSN](/guides/register-and-get-a-bsn/) for the differences. Foreign certificates often have to be legalised or carry an apostille, and that is done in the country that issued them. Check the rules for your country on [Netherlands Worldwide](https://www.netherlandsworldwide.nl/legalisation/foreign-documents). Documents that are not in Dutch, English, French or German need a sworn translation.
4. **Ask your employer about the 30% ruling.** If you are recruited from abroad, you may qualify for this tax benefit. Agree it with your employer before you start. See [The 30% ruling](/guides/30-percent-ruling/).

## In your first days

1. **Register with the municipality (*gemeente*).** If you will stay more than 4 months, register within 5 days of arriving. You go in person and you receive your citizen service number (*burgerservicenummer*, BSN). Almost everything else on this list needs it. See [Register and get a BSN](/guides/register-and-get-a-bsn/).
2. **Collect your residence permit, if you need one.** The letter from the immigration service (IND) tells you where and when. See [Residence permits](/guides/residence-permits/).

## In your first weeks and months

1. **Open a Dutch bank account.** Banks ask for your BSN, so do this after you register. See [Open a bank account](/guides/bank-account/).
2. **Take out Dutch health insurance.** If you have to insure yourself here, do it no later than 4 months after you arrive. If you are on time, the cover counts from the day you arrived. If you are late, you are not covered for the time before, and you can be fined. See [Health insurance](/guides/health-insurance/).
3. **Apply for a DigiD.** This is your login for government websites, including the tax office and the rent tribunal. You need a BSN, a registered address and a mobile phone. The activation code comes by post to your registered address. The Dutch DigiD page says the letter is sent within 5 working days. The English page says within 3 business days in one place and within a maximum of 5 working days in another. Count on up to 5 working days. See [DigiD](/guides/digid/).
4. **Apply for the 30% ruling.** You and your employer apply together. Do it within 4 months of your first working day, so that it counts from the start. See [The 30% ruling](/guides/30-percent-ruling/).
5. **Learn how the supermarket works.** You pay a deposit on bottles and cans, plastic bags are never free, and supermarkets do not sell spirits or tobacco. Compare the price per kilo or litre, not the pack price. See [Do your grocery shopping](/guides/groceries/).

## What depends on what

| To do this | You first need |
| --- | --- |
| Register with the municipality | An address, your ID and your certificates. A residence permit or MVV if you are not an EU, EEA or Swiss citizen |
| Get a BSN | To register with the municipality |
| Open a bank account | A BSN |
| Take out health insurance | A BSN |
| Apply for a DigiD | A BSN, a registered address and a mobile phone |
| Apply for the 30% ruling | A job contract and an employer who applies with you |

If one step is stuck, check this table first. The usual cause is a missing address or a missing BSN.
